The science behind movement

Meet BDNF.
A protein that helps the brain adapt.

Brain-derived neurotrophic factor, or BDNF, supports the survival and function of nerve cells and the plasticity of their connections. It is involved in the biology of learning and memory.

Exercise can temporarily increase BDNF measured in the blood. Researchers are studying how this response relates to the brain’s ability to adapt—and how exercise may support healthy aging and rehabilitation.

A rise in blood BDNF is a biological signal, not proof of brain repair. It does not guarantee better memory, prevent dementia, or predict an individual’s recovery.

An option for appropriate participants / HIIT

Adapted HIIT:
Endurance for daily life.

High-intensity interval training alternates challenging work with easier movement or rest. The intensity is relative to your fitness and health—not somebody else’s speed.

In a small study of 12 volunteers, short bouts of high-intensity cycling produced a larger immediate circulating BDNF response than prolonged light cycling. This is promising early evidence, not a universal workout prescription. Read the study ↗

HIIT can also be a way to challenge aerobic fitness. For selected adults with chronic stroke, a supervised trial found greater improvements in walking capacity with vigorous interval walking than with moderate training. Those findings apply to the screened participants and supervised program studied. Read the walking trial ↗

Personalized progression

Start at your level.
Build toward your goals.

Your starting point may be seated strength work, supported standing, walking practice, or aerobic exercise. HIIT is an optional tool when appropriate—not a requirement for wellness.

Start with your starting point

Review your goals, health history, recent hospitalization, medications, balance, and current mobility. New weakness or difficulty walking calls for assessment to determine whether skilled rehabilitation is needed before wellness training.

Build gradually

Discuss a plan that combines conditioning, strength, and recovery. Effort, symptoms, and movement quality guide progression; heart-rate targets may need adjustment for medications or medical conditions.

Measure what matters

Focus on practical changes: activity tolerance, strength, walking capacity, and confidence. A wellness plan should serve your life—not chase a BDNF number.

After a stroke or with heart, lung, or other significant medical conditions, discuss vigorous exercise with your healthcare team before starting. Stop exercise and seek medical attention for chest pain, fainting, or unusual severe breathlessness.

A little more clarity

Your questions, answered.

Can I participate if I have Medicare?

We cannot currently provide Medicare-covered services to patients with Medicare. We can offer only services Medicare does not cover, such as general wellness and fitness programs, on a private-pay basis. Skilled maintenance therapy can qualify for Medicare coverage and should not be treated as noncovered wellness simply because it focuses on maintaining function. Contact us before scheduling so we can confirm whether your requested service is appropriate.

Can you help me work toward walking again after hospitalization?

Start with a physical therapy evaluation to understand why walking has become difficult and what support is appropriate. A plan may address strength, balance, transfers, endurance, and walking practice. Regaining walking is an individualized goal, not a guaranteed outcome; wellness can support ongoing activity when clinically appropriate.

What does STRIVE’s wellness program focus on?

STRIVE’s wellness approach centers on everyday movement and independence, with attention to neurological needs and changes in mobility. Goals may include getting up from a chair, moving around the home, continuing exercise after therapy, or building stamina for community outings. We help determine whether wellness support or skilled physical therapy fits your needs.

Where does STRIVE serve clients?

STRIVE serves Las Vegas, Summerlin, Centennial Hills, Skye Canyon, and surrounding communities. Contact us to discuss your location, current availability, and options for personalized support.

Is STRIVE wellness private-pay?

Yes. STRIVE’s wellness services are private-pay. Contact us for current rates and service options before scheduling.

How do I get started?

Contact STRIVE to discuss your goals, current activity level, and whether wellness training or a physical therapy evaluation is the appropriate next step.
